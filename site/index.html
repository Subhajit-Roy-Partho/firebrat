Why it's different

A plain text-to-speech pass destroys a technical book. Firebrat doesn't.

Most audiobook tools treat a PDF as a wall of text to flatten into speech. That breaks the moment a book says "as shown in Figure 6.65" or writes out an equation.

Formulas, spoken and shown

An LLM recognizes the mathematical relationship in the page text, writes real narration for it, and Firebrat renders and highlights the actual formula on screen at the exact moment it's spoken.

Every figure stays visible

The diagram currently being described gets emphasized — a glow, a border — but every other figure and table for that section stays in view and tappable. Nothing is ever hidden from you.

Free to wander, never stuck

Tap any sentence to jump straight there. Skip forward, skip back, jump sections — always, regardless of whether autoplay is on. Autoplay is a convenience layered on top, never a constraint.

Fully offline once downloaded

Download or import a book once and the reader never needs a network again. Books are ordinary portable .tar.gz files — copy one to a phone over USB and open it directly, no server required at all.

Text sized for you

Adjustable text scale, high-contrast highlighting, and short, sentence-length segments are core design decisions from day one — not accessibility bolted on after the fact.

Locally-run narration

Narration is synthesized with an open-source TTS model running on your own hardware — no per-minute cloud TTS bill, no audio ever leaving the machine that converts the book.

Under the hood

One command, three stages, a portable book

The full technical breakdown lives on the how it works page — here's the shape of it.

1

Extract

Layout-aware parsing pulls out text, figures, and tables from the PDF, page by page.

2

Compile

An LLM groups pages into sections, writes clean narration, and identifies formulas worth showing.

3

Narrate

Local TTS synthesizes each sentence, timed to the millisecond against the assembled audio track.

4

Package

Everything is bundled into one .tar.gz — download it, import it, or share it directly.
